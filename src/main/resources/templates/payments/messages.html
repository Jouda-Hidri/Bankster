Payment messages

ISO 20022 replaced a generation of formats because it carries structured data where the old ones carried free text, uses one model across initiation, clearing and reporting, and returns standard reason codes instead of prose. The MT103 below is shown alongside for contrast.

pain.001 — customer credit transfer initiation

What a customer sends its bank to instruct payments. Two details carry real weight: CtrlSum is checked by the receiving bank, so a truncated file is rejected whole rather than executed in part; and LclInstrm/Cd set to INST is how the instant rail is actually requested — the same message without it is a standard transfer.

camt.053 — bank to customer statement



    The external record reconciliation runs against. EndToEndId is what
    makes automatic matching possible — it is the payer's own reference, preserved unchanged by
    every institution in the chain. Matching on amount and date alone fails as soon as two
    payments of the same amount happen on the same day.






MT103 — the legacy cross-border format



    Positional and tag-driven. :32A: carries value date, currency and
    settled amount; :33B: the original instructed amount before
    conversion; :36: the rate applied; :71A:
    who bears the charges. Amounts use a comma as the decimal separator and no thousands
    separator. Remittance information gets one field of 140 characters — which is the whole
    argument for migrating to ISO 20022.
